The register, alone
Iteration 43 read web first as the whole product on the web, and drew a group leader in a phone browser. The correction is smaller and sharper: the first build is one role, not one surface. The app is a separate build, so the office is the only person with anywhere to go.

Who gets what
Stated plainly, because three of these four rows are the reason the release is small enough to finish.
The congregation Nothing. No app, no page, no message — they are recorded, and they are not told. With the app
A guest Nothing. There is no way to find this church and no way to apply to it. With the app, or a public page
A member Nothing. Cannot see their own record, cannot correct it, cannot be asked anything. With the app
The administrator Everything in this release: the register, the households, the groups, import, export, and who else may administer. Now
Why the register is the one slice that can ship alone
Every other flow in these 93 artboards is two-sided. Take away three of the four roles and each one loses the half that starts it.
Applications, W1 needs an applicant. Nobody can apply, so the queue has no input and no first row.
RSVP and hosting, M8 M9 needs a member with a button. Without one there is nothing to answer and nothing to count.
Attendance, M14 needs a leader in the room. Nobody is holding a phone in the hall on Wednesday.
Statements, W14 W15 W16 needs a person to state something. Iteration 42 gave them a store; this release gives it no writer.
The week, W8 needs a reader. The office can compose it and nothing can display it.
The church code, W4 needs an app to scan it into. A code that resolves to nothing is a poster with no door behind it.
Pendataan is the only one-sided thing in the product. The office types it and the office reads it, so it works with an audience of one. It is also the thing the brief named first — and iteration 26 caught the staging having quietly put it last.
Dormant is not deleted
Nine office screens and every mobile artboard stay in this canvas, designed and argued, waiting for the surface that feeds them. Nothing above is a cut — it is a sequencing decision, and the order it implies is the reverse of the usual one: the institution gets its tool first, and the congregation gets theirs when the app ships.
What this drops from iteration 43
The offline queue. It existed for a leader ticking names in a concrete hall. No leader, no hall, no service worker — the single most expensive item on the last sheet is gone.
The forwardable link leak. Sign-in links go to two administrators, not to 248 people, so the sixth leak on S10 shrinks back to an ordinary session question.
Push, and the app shell. Neither was carrying anything once S7 moved act-now messages to WhatsApp.
Two of three roles. W13 becomes a list of administrators, and the two-administrator rule becomes the whole of access control.
Decide anyway, or pay a migration
Two stores, one writer each. The archive has its writer; the statement does not exist yet. Build one store now and iteration 42 becomes a rewrite of every row.
One global person table. Decision 7 on S5, and still the only one that cannot be retrofitted.
Household and family are different things. S14 settles which one this release stores, and the answer changes the schema rather than a screen.
A service is an entity. Nothing in the register displays one, and attendance will hang off it the moment the app lands.
The risk this choice takes
Iteration 17 turned on the office this time: a register with no weekly rhythm goes stale. Lidya types 248 people over two evenings, and then has no reason to open it again until somebody moves house.

The new interest fields cut into that: 12 people asked to join a care group and none are placed is a queue the office can work through without anybody else being online. It is the first thing in this release that generates its own next task.
