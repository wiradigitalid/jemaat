One decision, and it shapes the pilot
The staging question and the warta question turned out to be the same question. Here are both answers as an ordinary member would live them — not a leader, not a host, not the office. The person the pilot is actually about.
M28 Without the warta
R0 exactly as staged in iteration 22.
TUE Nothing.
WED Taps Going. Two seconds.
THU Nothing.
FRI Nothing.
SAT Goes to church. The app played no part.
1 opening a week, for about two seconds
WHAT THE PILOT THEN PROVES
Whether LEADERS keep marking attendance for three months. A fair question, and a cheaper release — but not the question anyone thinks they are asking.
M29 With the warta
The same twelve screens, plus M20 and one row on Home.
TUE Nothing.
WED Taps Going. Two seconds.
THU The week lands. Reads three announcements, sees who is speaking Saturday.
FRI A birthday. Sends Melisa a greeting in one tap.
SAT Checks the time and the speaker on the way.
4 openings a week, one of which reaches another member
WHAT THE PILOT THEN PROVES
Whether a CONGREGATION adopts it. That is what a pilot is for, and it is the only version that makes R1 launch into an installed base rather than a cold start.
The cost is one mobile screen, not a module
W8 is already in R0, and W8 already collects the announcements, the speaker and the sermon link — the office is already doing the typing either way. The question was never whether to build a feature. It is whether to show data the product is already gathering.

Recommendation: take it. Unless the pilot’s real purpose is to test whether leaders sustain attendance-taking — in which case the left-hand column is the correct instrument and should be chosen deliberately, not by omission.
