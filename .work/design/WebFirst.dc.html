Web first, no app
The decision is to go live on the web and leave the app for later. It is cheaper than it looks, and the reason is that two earlier iterations already did the expensive thinking: S7 sent everything a person must act on to WhatsApp, and S8 found that the congregation therefore needs nothing installed. Drop the app and exactly one person is affected.

What the app was quietly carrying
Nobody wrote these down as features, which is exactly why they get discovered in week three.
The session Held for months. Signing in was a first-run event nobody repeated. A link in WhatsApp, and it stays signed in on that phone. B1 keeps M11’s promise anyway: it asks again after 12 days before showing anyone else’s number.
The install An icon, from a store, with a download nobody in R0 was going to do. Add to home screen — B6. And the trap that will hit every leader once: from inside a message browser there is nothing to add, so B5 exists to say so.
Push Assumed, until S7 examined it and moved everything urgent to WhatsApp. Gone, and it costs almost nothing. Nice-to-know notifications disappear; everything a person must act on was already going down a channel they read.
The offline write M17 was designed: the duty card on the phone, a tapped RSVP queued rather than lost. A service worker and a queue, and this one is real work. Attendance is marked inside a concrete hall with no signal — see B3 — and it is the write everything else depends on.
A sixth leak for S10, and this one is mine
The app never had a forwardable front door. A link that carries a session does: forward the message and you have handed over the account. So a tokened link opens one page, for a short time, and never the directory — B4 says that on the screen rather than in a policy. A link that signs someone in is a different object with a different lifetime, and it dies on first use (W18).
What it saves
One codebase. Go and React, and the Flutter shell is not written, not reviewed, not shipped twice.
No install funnel. S8 named it the largest adoption lever in the design. Web-only takes it to zero for everyone, not just the congregation.
No store review. A fix reaches the pilot church the afternoon it is written, which is the whole point of a pilot.
S9’s two phone screens stop being a cost and become the same route at a narrower width.
What it costs
The offline queue has to be built. Days, not hours, and it is not optional — see the box below.
Every leader meets the message-browser dead end once, on the day they are onboarded. B5 is the whole answer.
No push at all until a page is saved to the home screen, and on iOS not even then unless it is. Mitigated by S7, not by us.
A shared phone is now a real case. B1 offers the code instead of the link, and W18 defaults to signing out when the browser closes.
O2’s QR scan needs camera permission in a browser — already Later on S1, so nothing moves.
The one honest reason to refuse
A group leader on a cheap Android inside a concrete hall with no signal is the worst case there is for a web app — and it. 17 established that he is the load-bearing volunteer: when he stops ticking, RSVP numbers, M23 and every count in the product stop with him.

So web first is safe only if the offline queue ships with B3, not after it. Ship attendance that loses a tick in a hall and the pilot fails at the exact thing it exists to test — and it will look like the design was wrong, when it was the surface.
