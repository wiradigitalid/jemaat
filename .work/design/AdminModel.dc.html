Household, family, and the address
The question that produced this sheet: is a household who lives in one house, or is it a family with a head? They are two different facts and the design had been using one word for both. A household is an address with people in it. A family is who those people are to each other. One changes when somebody moves; the other does not.

Two facts, and what each one owns
THE FACT WHAT IT HOLDS WHEN IT CHANGES
Household The address, the map pin, and the people living at it. One of them is the head. Somebody moves in or out. A move rewrites the household, never the person.
Family Who a person is to another person: spouse, parent, child. A marriage, a birth, a death. Never a move — a married daughter is still a daughter.
What ships now is the household, with a relationship written on each occupant. That is the shape of a Kartu Keluarga, it is what a church prints as a family card, and it answers the visitation question a register is actually asked. Kinship between households — a daughter who married out — stays a link the office can follow, not a graph the register maintains.
One list of relations, and where the line falls
Not everyone under a roof is kin. The relation is how a person is attached to the household, so it covers both — and whether they are family is derived from the value, never asked as a second question.

W26 shows the two groups as two blocks, so a reader sees at a glance who is family and who simply lives here. A helper is in the household and not in the family — she is on the visitation list, she is not on the family card, and her own family lives in Sukabumi where this register cannot see it.
The situations that decide the model
Every one of these is ordinary in an Indonesian congregation, and each one breaks a design that has only one of the two facts.
A married son and his wife live in his parents’ house Two households at one address, not one household of six. Each has its own head, and W26 shows the neighbour at the foot of the address panel.
A daughter marries and moves out She leaves the household and joins a new one. The old household keeps her in Moved out, with where she went — which is the kinship link, kept without a kinship table.
A student boards with a church family An occupant of that household whose relationship is Boards here. No kinship at all, and the visitation list is still right.
A widow lives alone A household of one, and she is its head. A head is a role, never a rank, and never a man by default.
A person works in another city Household unchanged. Their own address overrides the household’s, which is the only reason a person carries an address field at all.
Parents divorce, children split the week One household holds the children, the other is recorded on the parent. The register refuses to model custody, and the office writes what it needs in neither — there is no notes field.

One head, and it is a role
Exactly one occupant per household carries it, and the screen refuses a household with none rather than guessing. It decides who a letter is addressed to and who the office rings first — nothing else, and no permission anywhere hangs off it.
The address lives on the household
Decision 6 on S5, unchanged: correct it once and the whole house moves. The person-level override exists for the student in a dorm and the worker in another city, and it is left empty on almost every record.
The pin is the truth, not the text
Indonesian addresses defeat geocoders: a gang with no name, a blok that repeats, an RT that only the neighbours know. So the office searches, then drags the pin, and the coordinates are what a visitation team actually follows.
