Jemaat
A church membership and care group app for Indonesian congregations. One app serving many churches, each found by its own code. Mobile for everyone, a web side for the work that genuinely needs a big screen, and WhatsApp for anything a person has to act on.

Ten positions, each one chosen rather than inherited
These are the places the design says no to something a competitor ships. Each is defensible and each is reversible — but reverse one knowingly, not by accident.
The worship day is a setting, not Saturday
The pilot church meets on Saturday. A Sunday church changes one field and no screen notices
Institutional vocabulary never appears before someone is inside
P5 asks about the situation, not about Community versus Registered. A form that asks an unanswerable question gets closed
There is no rejection screen, anywhere
The furthest the office can go is asking for a conversation. A church does not refuse someone by push notification
No count, no percentage, no score — in the UI or the schema
A column that exists is eventually shown. M23 refuses one, and S5 has no attendance_rate to be tempted by
Nothing pastoral is automated down any pipe
A leader chasing you is care. A robot chasing you is not, and the difference is the whole product
Home shows only what is due
Most weeks most members are due nothing, and Home should then be nearly empty. Empty is a correct state
The app is optional for ordinary members
WhatsApp reply buttons carry RSVP, serving and hosting. The office needs the web, the leader needs the app, the congregation needs nothing
Serving is not a status, and leading is not a rank
Grace leads a care group while still a Guest. The screen shows her standing so nobody tries to correct it
The register is the church’s, and it can always leave
Export is on every Tuesday, not on the way out. MIT, self-hostable, and the JSON holds nothing back
No public directory of churches using Jemaat
The obvious growth feature. It would publish a national index of congregations with their addresses and meeting times
How it ships
R0 · 15 screens, 7 capabilities. One pilot church, accounts by hand, no public area. The care group week works end to end and the church enters its own register.

R1 · 13 screens, 4 capabilities. Any church can be found, join itself and import its people. This is where it becomes a product.

R2 · 4 screens. The promises the first two made.

Sixteen more are parked with a reason each, and the biggest single cut is the whole church-wide serving module — the volunteer rotation that was asked for lives inside a care group, and M9 already does it.
Two decisions still open
M20, the weekly warta. Beyond the original brief, and the only thing giving an ordinary member a reason to open the app between meetings. S2 shows both versions of their week. The office already types the content either way.

M23, the noticing prompt. Pastoral care or attendance surveillance depending entirely on how a church holds it. S3 separates what the design enforces from what it cannot, and recommends shipping the capability with the assumption switched off.
Known to be missing
None of this is in the repo corpus yet. It lives in a scratch folder, so a coding worker cannot be pointed at it.
Seventeen decisions are stated on screens and in notes, but no DEC- has been written. A decision nobody registered is a decision that quietly disappears in week three.
Spacing was never systematised the way colour and type were. S6 derives a scale from what the screens use; it needs enforcing in code.
Time zones are named on W12 and applied nowhere. Every screen says 19.30 and none says which.
Bad exits — discipline, safeguarding, someone who must not be assigned — never examined. It needs the children’s ministry module, which is not in any release.
